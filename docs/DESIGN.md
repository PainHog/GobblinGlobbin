# Don't Get Gobble Gobbled (working title): design document

This is a living document. **§1 Pitch** is Richard's own. **§3 Decisions** lists only what Richard has approved. Everything in §4–§6 is undecided until he chooses.

## 1. Pitch (Richard Moore, 2026-10-26)

> **Game name:** Don't Get Gobble Gobbled
>
> **Concept:** Players are the turkeys that will go on the table tonight, one lucky or unlucky turkey will get gobbled. Its a party escape concept, hiding trying not to gobble like a turkey and give themselves away, its a hide and seek style ttrpg. The players could always just leave a turkey behind, but thats not how turkeys roll.

### What the pitch settles
- **The players are turkeys** due on the table tonight.
- **One turkey will get gobbled,** a "lucky or unlucky" one.
- **A party escape game:** hide-and-seek style, set over one night or one dinner.
- **The core tension: don't gobble.** A gobble gives you away.
- **The theme is loyalty:** the flock could leave a turkey behind, "but that's not how turkeys roll".

## 2. Name check (2026-10-26; risk flags, not legal advice)

- **No game or product called "Don't Get Gobble Gobbled" or "Don't Get Gobbled" turned up.** The phrase "don't get gobbled" is used loosely in marketing copy, but not as a title. **The name looks clear so far.** Run a full search (DriveThruRPG, itch.io, Steam, Amazon toys and games, the USPTO and the UK IPO) before any store page, cover or logo.
- **Avoid "Gobblet":** *Gobblet* and *Gobblet Gobblers* are a different, branded board game from Blue Orange Games. "Gobble" itself is a common word.
- **It fits a series.** The title echoes Richard's *Don't Get Forked*. A "Don't Get…" line of party one-shots could be a deliberate brand. That's Richard's call; the decision is in §5.
- **The turkey niche is busy with small, mostly one-page games:**
  - *The Last Thanksgiving*: turkeys against the Chef, one page.
  - *Time Turkeys*: time-travelling turkeys. Notably, **making turkey noises gives +1** there, the opposite of this game's "don't gobble".
  - *Turkeys of Doom*: turkeys get revenge.
  - *The Turkey Trial*: an escape-room card game where you play a turkey.
  - *Party Fowl* and *The Gobbler*: D&D and Pathfinder adventures.
  - *Life on the Veg*: vegetables escaping dinner.

  **What sets this game apart:** live hide-and-seek, the gobble you're trying to hold in, and the no-turkey-left-behind loyalty. Make those the centrepiece.

## 4. Early flags: where the pitch meets the lessons in `DESIGN-RULES.md`
These aren't decisions. They're the first things to settle, because each one has already caused trouble in an earlier game.

1. **Identity: this sits close to *Nine Lives Gang* and *Heisty Spideys*.** All three have small creatures in a house, humans and pets as seekers, and hiding. Decide early what makes it different: the hide-and-seek rhythm (seekers counting and searching), the gobble urge, the party format, and rescuing each other.
   - **Reuse what those games learned,** especially Nine Lives Gang's fixes:
     - getting chased out must never seal the house (A13–14);
     - define what happens when meters are full (A1);
     - the session's end must read correctly at the very start and the very end (A12);
     - trackers need enough rows (A28).
2. **"One turkey will get gobbled": player elimination at a party game.** A player who's out early sits and watches, which Heisty Spideys had to fix ("nobody sits out").
   - **Decide whether gobbling is inevitable** (one turkey always goes, so the question is which) or **a risk** (the flock can save everyone). And what "lucky or unlucky" means.
   - **Decide what a gobbled player does next.** Suggestions only: they become a seeker (the cook's helper, the dog, a kid), or a ghost turkey who can still help or haunt.
   - **Either way, being gobbled must cost something but never end the fun** (A4).
3. **The gobble urge is the heart of the game.** Build it as a track with every state defined (A1):
   - what raises it;
   - what lowers it;
   - what happens when it's full;
   - whether one event can trigger several gobbles (one event, one rise, A2);
   - whether it can spiral (gobbling draws seekers, which makes you more nervous, which makes you gobble more);
   - whether it applies to each turkey or the whole flock.

   **A party-game option** (a suggestion): a real-world rule, such as a player who actually says "gobble" or laughs at the wrong moment adds to their urge. It's fun, but it has to stay fair and optional.
4. **Leaving a turkey behind must be a real choice, not a trap** (A4, A6, A18).
   - If abandoning a turkey is always better, nobody rescues. If rescuing always fails, nobody tries.
   - Earlier games had failed rescues that cascaded into more captures. Make loyalty worthwhile, and say plainly what a rescue costs.
5. **Seekers need procedures, not scripts** (A25): the cook, the family, the kids, the dog, the cat.
   - How each one searches, sees, hears and catches; where they look; and what "found" means.
   - **Hiding spots must not run out, or "the nearest one" must handle "there is none"** (A13–14).
   - **Group hiding:** several turkeys in one spot.
6. **A clock that binds** (A22): dinner time, the oven timer, the guests arriving. It should pressure every game, not just exist.
7. **Party-game pacing:**
   - **Players:** decide the count. Party games often go to 6–8; test every size (A21).
   - **Setup:** under 15–30 minutes, with ready-made turkeys (A27).
   - **Turns:** short, so nobody waits long.
   - **The Storyteller's round** fits on one card (A29).
8. **Tone, age and the holiday:**
   - **Tone:** a family-friendly comedy, where "gobbled" means eaten, played as a cartoon (art checklist §24).
   - **Holiday:** US Thanksgiving only, or any turkey dinner (Canadian Thanksgiving, Christmas)?
   - **No "Pilgrims and Indians" imagery** (art checklist §25).

## 5. Open questions for the design interview (DESIGN-RULES Part B, matched to this game)
1. **Identity:**
   - **Format:** tone, age range, number of players, session length.
   - **The holiday framing:** Thanksgiving only, or any turkey dinner.
   - **The series question:** is this part of a "Don't Get…" series with *Don't Get Forked*?
   - **The title:** keep it, or adjust it.
2. **Who gets gobbled:** inevitable or a risk; what "lucky or unlucky" means; what the gobbled player does after (flag 2).
3. **Engine.** Reuse a proven engine or build new, with the trade-offs for a fast party game:
   - Heisty Spideys' d6 pool;
   - Don't Get Forked's trait die plus a risk die;
   - Nine Lives Gang's push-your-luck;
   - a lighter party mechanic (cards, tokens, a dice-chucking urge).
4. **The turkeys:**
   - ready-made or built characters;
   - what makes each turkey play differently (traits, a knack, a nervous habit that sets off a gobble);
   - duplicates;
   - a quick-pick option.
5. **The gobble urge:** the track, triggers, calming down, a full urge, the flock versus each turkey, and any real-world party rule (flag 3).
6. **Hiding and seeking:**
   - the seekers and their procedures;
   - noticing and searching;
   - hiding spots (types, capacity, whether they run out);
   - moving between spots;
   - line of sight and noise;
   - group hiding (flag 5).
7. **The escape:** the goal (out of the house, to the woods, past the gate), ways out (two or more per map), obstacles, and an escape that doesn't need the athletic turkey.
8. **Loyalty and rescue:** what rescuing costs and gains, and why leaving a turkey behind is tempting but costly (flag 4).
9. **The clock and the threat:** dinner time, the seekers' alert, every state at its limit, and one event, one rise.
10. **Failure:**
    - spotted, cornered, caught, stuffed, gobbled;
    - getting free again;
    - whether you can give up;
    - what happens if everyone is caught;
    - what the gobbled player does.
11. **The end:** every way a session ends, read literally at the start and end. Result tiers: the whole flock escapes, a partial escape, a feast. Any sequel or holiday carry-over?
12. **Party size:** every supported player count, with adjustments.
13. **Ready-made content:** houses or farms with maps and room tables (two or more ways in and out), the seekers' routines, and a house generator if any (A25–A26).
14. **The first night:** dice and props, sheets, ready-made turkeys, the seeker tracker (enough rows) and a round card (A27–A29).
15. **Simulator targets:** escape and gobble rates per difficulty and party size; how often a rescue is attempted and succeeds; game length.
16. **Art direction** (cartoon style; each turkey's look) and **Foundry** (from day one, or book first?).

## 6. Suggestions (for discussion, not decisions)
- **Make the gobble the star.** A visible, rising urge that every player feels, with funny, tactical ways to calm it (breathing, distracting yourself, a friend's wing over your beak).
- **Lean into the hide-and-seek rhythm.** A seeker "counting" phase, then a search phase, with the flock moving between hiding spots, fits a party game better than long scene-by-scene play.
- **Use one-turkey-will-be-gobbled as the dramatic question**, but give the flock ways to cheat fate. For example, a decoy (a dressed-up butterball, a tofurky) that can take the place of a gobbled turkey if the flock pulls it off.
- **If it's part of a "Don't Get…" series,** a shared trade dress (title lettering, page frames) and a shared core engine across one-shots would build the brand.
