Chapter 10

Puddlewick Farm

Puddlewick Farm is wet. A creek runs right through the middle of it, and the only ways across are the old footbridge and a splashy ford by the duck pond. There’s a water mill, a greenhouse full of tomatoes, a goat called Old Gertie who has opinions, and mud. So much mud.

The farmer and the dog

Farmer Puddlewick wears bright yellow waders all year round and squelches wherever he goes. Pickles is a very long, very low dachshund with a very good nose.

The pranks

  • Hide the roasting pan. No pan, no roast. It’s under the porch swing now, probably.
  • Let the goats loose. Old Gertie leads a daring escape, and the farmer spends his whole turn chasing goats.
  • Start a hen party. Twenty hens, all talking at once. The noise carries across the creek.
  • Turn on the sprinklers. Every sprinkler in the greenhouse goes off at once. The farmer runs in to turn them off and comes out soaked.
  • Jam the water wheel. A well-placed stick, and the mill grinds to a halt. That’s going to take a while to fix.
  • Gobble up the pie berries. No berries, no pie. Grandma will be furious, and the farmer has to go and pick more.
  • Start the duck choir. Quack. QUACK. QUAAAACK. Very loud, very out of tune.
  • Dress a hay bale as a turkey. Two pinecone eyes and a red sock for a snood. It’s not great, but it only has to work once.

Playing Puddlewick Farm

  • Clock adjustment: the clock starts 1 higher than Chapter 8’s table says.
  • The creek cuts the farm in two. North of it there are only two ways back: the Footbridge and the ford at the Duck Pond. Don’t get stuck on the wrong side.
  • The Berry Patch is a dead end: one path in, one path out, the same one.
  • The Muddy Lane is open ground in the middle of everything.
  • Puddlewick is a little kinder to big flocks and harder on small ones: with only 2 turkeys, play it on Easy or Standard.
