Chapter 1

It’s Thanksgiving. Hide!

It’s Thanksgiving morning on the farm. The sun is up, the frost is melting off the pumpkins, and inside the farmhouse the farmer is pulling on his boots and humming. The oven is warming up. The family is coming at four. The farmer is looking for a turkey. Any turkey.

You are a turkey.

In Don’t Get Gobble Gobbled, each player is one turkey in a flock of friends. You can’t fight the farmer, and you can’t outrun him for long. What you can do is hide in the hayloft, sneak behind the cows, tip over the milk pails, let the goats loose and generally waste his entire morning, until it’s far too late to roast anybody. Then the family eats something else, and the flock struts off into the sunset.

There’s just one problem. You’re a turkey, and when turkeys get nervous, they gobble.

How you win

The Dinner Clock counts down toward Too Late: the moment it’s too late to cook a turkey in time for dinner. When it gets there, the farmer gives up, and the whole flock wins. Every round moves the clock on, and every prank you pull moves it on further.

How you lose

If the farmer grabs one turkey, the game ends at once. That turkey gets gobbled (this is a cartoon: it goes into the kitchen with a very surprised look on its face), and the whole flock loses, together. You could leave a slow friend behind to save your own feathers, but that’s not how turkeys roll, and it wouldn’t help: in this game, nobody wins alone.

Nobody is ever knocked out early, either. Everyone plays right to the end, because the end comes for all of you at once.

What kind of game is this?

A party game with a little roleplaying in it. Each player runs one turkey: what it does, what it says, and how loudly it gobbles. One more person is the Storyteller, who runs the farmer, the farm dog, the farmer’s kid and the farm itself by following simple tables (Part Two). The Storyteller isn’t playing against you; the dice and the tables decide what the farmer does. The Storyteller’s job is to make him loud, silly and just a little bit scary.

  • Players: 2 to 8 turkeys, plus the Storyteller.
  • Ages: 8 and up. Younger turkeys can play with a grown-up helping them read.
  • Time: about 45 minutes with 2 turkeys, up to about 2 hours with 8, including 15 minutes of setup (Chapter 8 has the details).
  • One night only: every game is complete in itself. Play it again next year on a different farm.

What you need

  • Ten six-sided dice (d6): 3 white action dice, 5 red gobble dice, 1 helper die in any other colour, and 1 die for the Storyteller (any colour). Ordinary board-game dice are perfect.
  • Printed from the back of this book: the map of the farm you’re playing (Chapters 9 to 11), a turkey card for each player, the Dinner Clock sheet, the tokens sheet, and the two reference sheets (At the Table, and the Storyteller’s Card).
  • Something to mark things with: a coin or paperclip for each turkey’s urge, a coin for the clock, and the cut-out tokens (or coins and buttons) for the turkeys, the farmer, the dog, the kid, the Feathers and the “Huh?” tokens.
  • A pencil, for ticking off pranks.

Chapter 12, Your First Night, walks through setting up and teaching the game.

The whole game in one breath

Every round, each turkey does one thing: sneak, hunker down, pull a prank, save a friend, or let out a gobble on purpose. Then the farmer walks, looks for turkeys, and maybe finds one. Rolls use white dice for what you’re trying to do and one red die for every point of your urge; any red 6 means you gobble, and a gobble tells the farmer where you are. If he spots you, your friends have one chance to save you before he grabs you. Get to Too Late with nobody grabbed, and you win.

The tone: slapstick

This is a comedy. The farmer trips over rakes. The goats get loose. A turkey hides inside a scarecrow and sneezes. When the game says “gobble”, it means dinner, but nothing in this book is ever gross or gory: being gobbled means a dramatic exit, a few last words, and a lot of mashed potatoes. Play it big, play it silly, and do the voices.

Party rules

Say it out loud

Whenever your turkey gobbles, you gobble too. Out loud. “GOBBLE GOBBLE!” Everyone else is allowed to gasp. This rule is always on.

Straight faces (optional)

Decide before you start whether to use this one. While the Storyteller is doing the farmer’s search (“Is there a turkey in the hayloft? Hmmm?”), turkeys are supposed to be very, very quiet. The first player to laugh out loud during a search raises their own turkey’s urge by 1. It only happens once per search, it never makes a turkey gobble at once (if its urge is already 5, it simply stays at 5), and it’s best for groups who enjoy being silly at each other. Skip it with very young players.

Turkey talk

  • A tom is a grown-up male turkey: big, puffed up, with a beard on his chest.
  • A hen is a female turkey: smaller, and usually the sensible one.
  • A jake is a young male, still growing into his gobble.
  • The floppy bit over the beak is the snood. It goes bright red when a turkey is excited. Today, it’s bright red a lot.

Designer’s Note

The best moments in this game happen when the farmer is right next to you, your urge is at 4, and you have to decide whether to stay perfectly still or make a run for it. Lean into those moments. Hold your breath. Look at the red dice. Then roll.
