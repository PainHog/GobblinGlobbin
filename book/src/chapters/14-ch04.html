Chapter 4

Don’t Gobble!

“The trick is not to think about gobbling.” “Now I’m thinking about gobbling.” “GOBBLE.”

Every turkey has an urge to gobble. It builds up all morning, and it gets much worse when the farmer is close. Too much urge, and a gobble slips out and tells him exactly where you are.

Your urge

Your urge is a number from 0 to 5, kept on your turkey card. Every turkey starts the game at urge 1 (it’s Thanksgiving, after all). Your urge is how many red dice you roll (Chapter 3), so the higher it is, the more likely each roll is to end in a gobble.

When your urge rises

Your urge goes up by 1:

  • at the end of every round, unless you’re Spotted (you have bigger things to worry about);
  • at a Close Call: when the farmer, or the dog, ends his turn in your zone and you aren’t Spotted (Chapter 5);
  • when you Puff Up for an extra white die (Chapter 3);
  • when your quirk says so;
  • when a farm event says so (Chapter 7).

One thing, one rise

Apart from Puffing Up and farm events, your urge rises at most once per action (yours or anyone’s), once per farmer’s turn and once at the end of each round, however many reasons there are. If the farmer and the dog both end up in your zone, that’s still one Close Call and +1. A farm event is its own thing: a backfiring truck raises everyone’s urge even if it just rose for something else, and so does anything the event sets off (a Jumpy turkey jumping at the gobbles it causes, say).

A full urge

Your urge can’t go above 5. If it’s at 5 and something would raise it, you gobble instead, and it goes back to 0. A full-urge gobble can’t be stopped with a Feather. Watch out: at urge 5 in the farmer’s zone, the Close Call at the end of his search makes you gobble, and that means Spotted. Hunker Down before you get there.

Calming down

The only way to lower your urge, apart from gobbling, is to Hunker Down (−2, Chapter 3), have a friend Hunker Down for you, or get lucky with a farm event. Urge never goes below 0.

Gobbling

However it happens, a gobble always does the same three things:

  1. Say it out loud. “GOBBLE GOBBLE!” (This is a rule.)
  2. Your urge goes to 0. Ahhh. That’s better.
  3. The farmer hears it. If he’s in your zone, you’re Spotted (Chapter 5). If he isn’t, put a “Huh?” token on your hiding spot, or on your zone if you’re in the open. He’ll come and have a look.

If a friend gobbles because of the helper die when Lending you a Wing, it’s your friend who gobbles, from your friend’s own spot.

Gobbling on purpose

Two actions are gobbles on purpose: Let It Out and Gobble for a Friend (Chapter 3). They work like any other gobble, except that Gobble for a Friend has its own rules about whom the farmer looks at.

Feathers

The flock shares a little pile of Feathers. A Feather is a friend’s wing clapped over your beak just in time.

  • After any roll, if a red die (or the helper die) shows a 6, the flock can spend one Feather to cancel that gobble. Nobody gobbles, no token goes down, and the urge stays where it is.
  • One Feather cancels one gobble. Feathers don’t come back once they’re spent.
  • A Feather can’t stop a gobble on purpose or a full-urge gobble. It can stop one of Pip’s hiccups, which come from a die.
  • The flock usually starts with 2 Feathers: Chapter 8 has the exact number for your flock size and difficulty.

Decide together whether to spend one. A gobble in the farmer’s own zone, which would make you Spotted, is nearly always worth a Feather. A gobble anywhere else resets your urge and sends him looking, and often that’s fine.

Example: near and far

Clementine is Pulling a Prank in the Duck Pond with urge 2, and the farmer is far away in the Farmhouse. Her roll gobbles. The flock keeps its Feathers: Clementine’s urge drops to 0, a “Huh?” token goes on the Reeds, and the farmer will start walking that way. That gives her a turn or two to move on.

Later, Noodle is hiding in the Hayloft when the farmer walks into the Big Barn and checks it. Noodle’s white dice say he stays hidden, but there’s a red 6. A gobble with the farmer in his zone means Spotted, so the flock spends a Feather straight away.

Designer’s Note

A gobble far from the farmer isn’t a disaster: it lets the steam out, and it can even pull him away from your friends. A gobble next to him is a disaster. Keep your urge low before you go anywhere near him, and Hunker Down before you have to.
