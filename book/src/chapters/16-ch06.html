Chapter 6

Pranks and the Dinner Clock

A turkey needs hours in the oven, and the family arrives at four. Every minute the farmer spends chasing goats, mopping up milk or looking for his hat is a minute closer to Too Late. Your job is to waste as many of his minutes as you can.

The Dinner Clock

The Dinner Clock is a countdown track on the Dinner Clock sheet. At the start of the game, put a coin on the space Chapter 8 gives you (with four turkeys on Standard at Bramble Farm, that’s 20).

  • At the end of every round, it moves down 1 space.
  • When a prank happens, it moves down by the prank’s number, straight away.
  • When it reaches 0, it’s Too Late. The farmer looks at his watch, throws his hat on the ground, and gives up. The flock wins at once, even in the middle of a turn, and even if somebody is Spotted.

Farm events

Every space that’s a multiple of 4 (24, 20, 16, 12, 8 and 4) is an EVENT space. Whenever the coin lands on one, or jumps past one, the Storyteller rolls a farm event (Chapter 7): a backfiring truck, a phone call from Grandma, a rake in the grass. Jump past two at once and there are two events, in order. Putting the coin on its starting space doesn’t count, even if that’s an EVENT space. If a prank moves the clock, roll its events before dealing with that roll’s gobble.

Pranks

Every farm has eight pranks, one in each of eight zones, shown with a star on the map. Each one lists:

  • its target, the roll you need to Pull the Prank (Chapter 3);
  • its boxes: how many times the roll has to work (a Great ticks two);
  • its clock number: how far the Dinner Clock moves when it happens;
  • anything else it does: loud, detour or decoy.

You can’t pull a prank while the farmer is in its zone. When the last box is ticked, the prank happens: move the clock, then do whatever else it says. Each prank happens only once; after that it’s done for the game. When every prank is done, the clock still ticks down by itself every round.

Loud

It makes a terrible racket. Put a “Huh?” token on the prank’s zone (not on a spot: when the farmer gets there, he rolls for which spot to check). Pull it, then get out of there.

Detour

It gives the farmer something urgent to deal with. On his next turn he doesn’t walk the paths: he goes straight to the prank’s zone and spends his turn there being busy (rounding up cows, chasing a rowboat). He doesn’t search, but turkeys there still have a Close Call, and any “Huh?” tokens in that zone come off. The dog and the kid carry on as normal. If two detours go off before his turn, he goes to the one that went off first; the other waits for the turn after.

Decoy

Each farm has one decoy: a scarecrow, a hay bale or a pumpkin, dressed up as a turkey. When it happens, tick Decoy ready on the Dinner Clock sheet. The next time the farmer would grab a turkey, he grabs the decoy instead: that turkey (and any other turkey he’d spotted there: his arms are full) isn’t Spotted any more, he carries the decoy all the way to the Farmhouse (put him there), the clock moves down 2, and he skips the rest of his own turn (no walking, searching or Close Calls). The dog and the kid still take theirs. Then the decoy is gone.

Example: turning off the oven

The Dinner Clock is at 14. Butterbean is in the Farmhouse, and the farmer is far away in the Cornfield. She Pulls a Prank: Turn off the oven, target 5+, two boxes, clock −3. Her urge is 1, so she rolls 2 white dice (Busy Beak) and 1 red: white 5, 2, red 3. It works, and Busy Beak makes the success count twice, so both boxes are ticked at once. The oven goes cold.

The clock jumps from 14 to 11. It passes the EVENT space on 12, so the Storyteller rolls a farm event.

Designer’s Note

A flock that only hides will usually be found in the end: the clock is long, and every round the farmer looks somewhere new. Pranks are risky, but they’re how you get to Too Late before he gets to you. The bigger your flock, the more you need them. Send your calmest turkeys, and keep everyone else out of the farmer’s way.

Too Late!

When the clock reaches 0, the farmer trudges back to the house, the family arrives, and someone has to make dinner out of whatever’s in the cupboard. The Storyteller rolls a d6 to see what they eat instead, while the flock does a victory strut:

d6What’s for dinner instead?
1Pizza, from the only place still open.
2Grandma’s famous tuna casserole. Nobody is sure about the crackers on top.
3A mountain of mashed potatoes, with lakes of gravy.
4Grilled cheese sandwiches for twenty-two.
5Breakfast for dinner: a tower of pancakes.
6The vegetable “turkey”: a cauliflower in a little paper hat.

Then the turkeys get the last word: each player says what their turkey does to celebrate.
