Chapter 3

A Round on the Farm

The game is played in rounds. In each round every turkey gets one go, then the farmer gets his, then the clock ticks on. Repeat until somebody gets grabbed or it’s Too Late.

The round

  1. The turkeys’ turn. Every turkey takes one action (below), in any order the flock likes. Talk it over: whoever is doing something risky usually goes first, so friends can help if it goes wrong. Each player decides what their own turkey does, and once a turkey has acted, it’s done for this turn.
  2. The farmer’s turn. The Storyteller moves the farmer, the dog and (in a big flock) the farmer’s kid, and they look for turkeys (Chapter 7). In round 1 the farmer is still indoors pulling on his boots, and there is no farmer’s turn at all. For the whole of round 1, he, the dog and the kid don’t count for anything: no Sneak needs a roll because of them, and nobody can be Spotted. Nobody may go into the Farmhouse in round 1, either. That’s your head start: scatter!
  3. The end of the round. The Dinner Clock moves down one space (Chapter 6); if that makes it Too Late, the flock wins and nothing else happens. If it lands on an EVENT space, the Storyteller rolls the farm event now. Then every turkey that isn’t Spotted raises its urge by 1 (Chapter 4), and Pip rolls for his hiccups.

The roll

Many actions need a roll. Every roll works the same way.

  • Take 1 white die. That’s your action die: it says whether you pull it off.
  • Take one red die for every point of your urge. At urge 0 you roll no red dice at all; at urge 5, five. These are your gobble dice.
  • Roll them all together.

Read the white dice. Every roll has a target, written like 4+. If your highest white die is at or above the target, it works; if not, it fails. Any white 6 is a Great: it works, and some actions give you a little extra.

Read the red dice. If any red die shows a 6, you gobble. It’s one gobble however many 6s there are. Chapter 4 says what a gobble does (nothing good).

The result comes first. Do what the white dice say (move, hide, tick a box), then deal with the gobble. If you sneak into the barn and gobble, you’re in the barn, gobbling.

There are no rolls against the farmer and no rolls to beat each other. The target is the only number that matters, and it never changes in the middle of a roll.

Boosts

A boost adds one white die to a roll. Roll all the white dice and use the highest. A roll never has more than 3 white dice, however many boosts you could get. There are three kinds:

Your knack

Most knacks give +1 white die on one kind of roll (Chapter 2).

Lend a Wing

A friend in the same zone as you can Lend a Wing on your roll: you add a white die, and your friend rolls the helper die (the one in the third color) along with yours. If the helper die shows a 6, your friend gobbles, not you. Lending a Wing doesn’t use your friend’s action (they can do it before or after their own go, and on rolls during the farmer’s turn), but:

  • only one friend can Lend a Wing on any one roll;
  • if your dice and the helper die both show a 6, you both gobble: that’s two gobbles, and it takes two Feathers to cancel them;
  • a friend who Lends a Wing during the farmer’s search is in his zone, so a 6 on the helper die makes the friend Spotted;
  • each turkey can Lend a Wing only once per round (turn your card sideways to remember);
  • a Spotted turkey can’t Lend a Wing.

Puff Up

Before you roll, you can Puff Up: raise your own urge by 1, and add a white die. Your new urge counts for this roll (one more red die), and it stays raised afterwards. You can’t Puff Up at urge 5, and you can’t Puff Up when you Gobble for a Friend.

Example: a roll

Pip is in the Big Barn with urge 2. Farmer Bramble is in the Cow Pasture, which is next to the Cornfield. Pip wants to Sneak to the Cornfield. The farmer is next to the zone Pip is entering, so it’s a roll, target 4+.

Pip’s knack, Soft Steps, gives him a second white die. He Puffs Up too (urge 2 to 3) for a third. He rolls 3 white dice and 3 red dice: white 2, 4, 1, red 3, 6, 1. His best white die is a 4: it works. He creeps into the Cornfield and hides in the Corn rows. But there’s a red 6: “Gobble… gobble?”

A “Huh?” token goes on the Corn rows, and Pip’s urge drops to 0. The flock could spend a Feather to cancel the gobble, but they decide not to: the farmer is coming this way anyway, and with urge 0 Pip will roll no red dice at all when he checks the Corn rows. Feathers are best saved for gobbles in the farmer’s own zone, which would make a turkey Spotted on the spot.

Actions

On the turkeys’ turn, each turkey does one of these. A Spotted turkey can only Scram.

Sneak

Move along a path to a zone next to yours. If a hiding spot there has room, you hide in it (you pick which); if not, you’re in the open.

  • Roll 4+ if the farmer is in or next to the zone you leave or the zone you enter. (“Next to” always means joined by a path.)
  • Otherwise roll 3+ if the zone you enter is Noisy (hens, ducks, sheep: they make a fuss).
  • Otherwise there’s nothing to roll: you just go.
  • If it fails, you still get there (and can still hide), but you’re noticed: if the farmer is in the zone you end in, you’re Spotted; otherwise put a “Huh?” token on your hiding spot, or on the zone if you’re in the open.
  • Great: you can keep going one more zone, if that move wouldn’t need a roll of its own.
  • Two zones at once (Pip’s Soft Steps): check every zone you leave or enter, the middle one too, and use the highest target. You can’t pass through the farmer’s zone. A Great still adds one more zone.

Hunker Down

Stay where you are and take some deep breaths. If you’re in the open and a spot in your zone has room, you hide in it first; if the farmer is in your zone, that takes a 4+ roll, and if it fails you’re Spotted. Then, whether or not you managed to hide, lower your urge by 2, or lower the urge of a friend in your zone by 2 instead. Urge never goes below 0.

You can’t move from one hiding spot to another in the same zone: to change spots, Sneak out.

When you Hunker Down, tell everyone how your turkey calms down. Counting feathers? Humming? A tiny bit of turkey yoga?

Pull a Prank

You must be in the zone where the prank is, and the farmer must not be. Roll; you need the prank’s target. If it works, tick one of its boxes; a Great ticks two. When all of a prank’s boxes are ticked, it happens (Chapter 6), and it’s done for the rest of the game. You stay where you are, hidden or not. Ticked boxes stay ticked, so friends can finish a prank you started.

Gobble for a Friend

The bravest thing a turkey can do. Pick a Spotted friend in your zone or a zone next to it, and gobble on purpose, as loudly as you can, so the farmer looks at you instead. Each Spotted turkey can be helped this way only once per round. If the rescuer ends up Spotted, that’s a new Spotted turkey, and another friend can try for it.

  • Your urge goes to 0. You roll no red dice and can’t Puff Up, but your knack and a friend’s Wing still count.
  • Roll 4+. If it works, your friend is no longer Spotted (it stays where it is).
  • Either way, the farmer turns toward you: if he’s in your zone, now you are Spotted; otherwise a “Huh?” token goes on your spot.
  • Great: he can’t tell where the gobble came from. No token, and you aren’t Spotted.

Scram

Only a Spotted turkey can Scram, and it’s the only thing a Spotted turkey can do. Roll 4+ (5+ on Hard). If it works, run along a path to a zone next door, hide in a spot there if one has room, and you’re no longer Spotted. A Great lets you keep running one more zone. If it fails, you’re still Spotted, and your friends had better help.

Let It Out

Sometimes you just have to. Gobble on purpose: your urge goes to 0, and a “Huh?” token goes on your spot (or your zone, if you’re in the open). The farmer will come and look. Do it where he’s far away, or do it to pull him away from your friends. Don’t do it in the same zone as him: that makes you Spotted.
