Difficulty and Flock Size

Before you start, pick a difficulty and count your turkeys. Together they set where the Dinner Clock starts, how many Feathers the flock gets, and who helps the farmer look.

The last column comes from playing tens of thousands of games by these rules with a computer, with a sensible flock that pranks when it’s safe and hides when it isn’t. A bolder or more careful flock will do a little better or worse, and a flock that learns to lead the farmer around with “Huh?” tokens will win more often. That’s the game working. If your table gets too good at it, move up a difficulty.

  • Easy is for a first game, young players, or a table that wants to laugh more than sweat.
  • Standard is the game as intended: you’ll usually win, and you’ll nearly always have a scare.
  • Hard is for experienced flocks. Expect to lose a turkey quite often, and expect it to be dramatic.

Big flocks: the farmer’s kid

With 5 or more turkeys, the farmer’s kid comes out to help (Chapter 7). A big flock has more friends to save each other and more beaks for pranks; the kid keeps it honest.

Small flocks

A flock of 2 or 3 turkeys gets the shorter clock and the extra Feather above. With only 2 turkeys there’s just one friend to save you, so every Spotted moment matters: with 2 turkeys, Hard wins only about half the time.

How long a game takes

A game lasts about 8 to 13 rounds. Allow about a minute for each turkey and two for the farmer every round, plus 15 minutes to set up and learn:

Choosing a farm

  • Bramble Farm (Chapter 9): the friendliest map, best for a first game.
  • Puddlewick Farm (Chapter 10): split by a creek, with chokepoints; best with 4 or more turkeys.
  • Windy Ridge Farm (Chapter 11): open hillsides where the farmer gets everywhere fast; for flocks that have played before.
