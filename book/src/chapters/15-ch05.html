Chapter 5

Hide and Seek

The farm is a big game of hide-and-seek, and the farmer is “it”. This chapter is about where you can hide, how he finds you, and what happens when he does.

The farm map

Each farm (Chapters 9 to 11) is split into twelve zones: the Big Barn, the Duck Pond, the Turkey Pen and so on. Your turkey is always in exactly one zone. Zones are joined by paths, shown on the map as orange gaps in the fence; you can only Sneak along a path. No path, no way through (that’s a fence, a creek or a very grumpy goose). Two zones are next to each other only when a path joins them.

  • Hiding spots. Most zones have a few places to hide: the Hayloft, the Corn rows, behind the cows. Each spot shows its cover, the roll you need to stay hidden when it’s searched (3+ is safe, 5+ is risky), and its room, how many turkeys fit (×4 means four).
  • Hidden or in the open. A turkey in a hiding spot is hidden. A turkey that isn’t (no room, or nowhere to hide) is in the open. Turkeys can share a spot until it’s full.
  • Open zones have no hiding spots at all: anyone there is in the open.
  • Noisy zones are full of hens, ducks, goats, sheep or pigs who make a fuss: Sneaking into one takes a 3+ roll even when the farmer is far away (Chapter 3).

Everyone looks at the map. The tables under each map (the farmer’s route and search lines) are for the Storyteller. They aren’t secret, but the farmer is funnier when he’s a surprise.

How the farmer looks for you

On his turn, the farmer walks, then searches the zone he ends in (the Storyteller has the exact steps in Chapter 7).

  • If a “Huh?” token is down somewhere, he heads for the nearest one, up to two zones a turn, and checks the spot it’s on.
  • If not, he wanders along his route and checks one hiding spot, chosen by a die roll.
  • When he searches, everyone in the open in the zone he ends in is Spotted. He doesn’t spot anyone in zones he only walks through.
  • Every turkey in a spot he checks rolls to stay hidden: the target is the spot’s cover. If the roll fails, or gobbles, that turkey is Spotted.
  • Turkeys in the zone that he didn’t find still have a Close Call: +1 urge.

“Huh?” tokens

A “Huh?” token is the farmer stopping, cupping a hand to his ear, and saying “Huh? What was that?”. It goes down when:

  • a turkey gobbles where the farmer isn’t (on its spot, or its zone if it’s in the open);
  • a Sneak fails (on the spot the sneaky turkey ends up in);
  • a turkey Gobbles for a Friend or Lets It Out (on its own spot);
  • the dog sniffs out a turkey, or the kid spots one (Chapter 7);
  • a loud prank goes off (on the prank’s zone, Chapter 6);
  • Duchess strikes a pose (her Show-off quirk, Chapter 2).

Tokens stay down until the farmer ends his turn in their zone and searches it. Then they all come off (except a pose Duchess strikes during that search). A turkey that Sneaks away from its token leaves the token behind, and the farmer will search an empty spot. That’s the idea.

The dog and the kid

On Standard and Hard, the farm dog wanders the farm and sometimes follows its nose toward the nearest turkey. It never grabs anyone, but if it ends its move in your zone you roll 4+ to stay hidden (it doesn’t care how good your spot is), and if you fail, it barks: a “Huh?” token on your spot. The dog gives you a Close Call too. The dog itself never makes anyone Spotted, but a gobble on its roll is an ordinary gobble: if the farmer is in your zone too, you’re Spotted.

With five or more turkeys, the farmer’s kid comes out to help Dad. The kid wanders, peeks into one hiding spot, and yells “DAD! TURKEY!” at any turkey it finds or sees in the open. That’s a “Huh?” token, not Spotted: the kid can’t grab you either.

Spotted!

Being Spotted means the farmer has seen you, and he’s coming. Turn your turkey token face down.

  • At the start of the farmer’s next turn, he grabs a Spotted turkey in his zone, and the game ends (below). If several are Spotted there, he grabs the one with the highest urge; on a tie, the Storyteller rolls a die to pick.
  • Until then, the flock has one chance to save you: the rest of the turkeys’ turn, if you were Spotted during it, or the whole next turkeys’ turn if you were Spotted on his turn or at the end of the round.
  • A Spotted turkey’s only action is Scram (Chapter 3). A Spotted turkey can’t Lend a Wing, and its urge doesn’t rise at the end of the round.
  • Friends can Gobble for a Friend (Chapter 3), once for each Spotted turkey each round.
  • You stop being Spotted when a Scram works, when a friend’s Gobble for a Friend works, or when the farmer isn’t in your zone at the start of his turn (Grandma called him away, or he carried off the decoy): he’s lost sight of you.

Grabbed

When the farmer grabs a turkey, the game ends at once and the whole flock loses. That turkey is gobbled: carried off to the kitchen, tucked under one arm, protesting loudly. Its player gets the last word (below). The only thing that can stop a grab is a decoy (Chapter 6).

Example: saving Duchess

Farmer Bramble wanders into the Big Barn and the Storyteller rolls his search line: the Hayloft. Duchess is in the Hayloft. She rolls to stay hidden, target 3+, with 2 white dice thanks to Still as a Stump (and her red dice don’t count): 1, 2. Spotted!

Next turkeys’ turn, Duchess tries to Scram first. Her urge is 1, so she Puffs Up (urge 2) for a second white die and rolls 2 white and 2 red: white 2, 3, red 4, 1. Still Spotted. Big Tom is next door in the Chicken Coop. He takes a deep breath and Gobbles for a Friend: his urge goes to 0, and he rolls 2 white dice (Big Gobble): 4, 6. A Great! The farmer spins round, can’t tell where that enormous gobble came from, and Duchess is no longer Spotted. Big Tom gets no “Huh?” token at all.

The farmer is still in the barn, though. Duchess should Sneak away next round, and she’ll need to roll for it.

Famous last gobbles

When a turkey gets grabbed, its player rolls a d6 (or makes up something better) for its last words as it’s carried off to the kitchen:
